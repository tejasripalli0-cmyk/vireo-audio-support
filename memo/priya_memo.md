# Support Ticket Analysis — Summary for Priya Raman

**To:** Priya Raman, Head of Customer Experience
**From:** Kabir Nanda's team
**Re:** Monthly ticket breakdown, and a check on the "most volume gets the hires" rule

## What we built

A tool that reads all 11,780 tickets (20 June 2024 – 30 June 2026) and assigns each
one a category from what the customer and the agent wrote, instead of relying
only on the chatbot's first-guess tag. It produces the monthly charts you asked
for, by category and by team. It costs nothing to run.

## What we found

Your number is right: 2,564 tickets (22%) carry the Billing tag, and Billing is
the second-largest team. The largest by raw volume is Chat Frontline (3,030,
26%).

**The most important finding:** when we re-read the text, only about **35%
(898)** of the Billing-tagged tickets look like billing issues. About **41%
(1,049)** look like delivery problems where the customer mentioned payment, and
the rest (617) are spread across other categories. This is our tool's reading,
not a verified fact.

## What it means for the two-hire rule

Raw volume alone is an incomplete basis. Teams differ in size (3 to 15 agents)
and in how long tickets take (about half an hour to over four days). Billing
handles the most tickets per agent (641, versus 202 for Chat), yet part of its
volume may belong to Logistics. Escalations & Warranty has the fewest tickets
but the longest handling times. **Our analysis does not prove where the two hires
should go**, and we are not recommending a team.

## A separate money finding

Policy gives customers an automatic ₹350 credit when a first response is late.
10.9% of tickets since the new helpdesk started (839 of 7,728) missed their
target, chat and email worst (about 12%). Applied to a full year, the policy
implies about **₹3.7 lakh a year (≈ ₹92,700 a quarter)**. This is an estimate of
what the policy commits to, not confirmed payments; the data does not show
whether credits are actually being paid. No target from Vireo exists, so we have
not set one.

## What is uncertain

- Our check of the tool used 150 tickets reviewed by the AI that built it, not by
  an independent person. It agreed on about 94% (141 of 150). Treat this as a
  first pass.
- The 35% / 41% split is the tool's reading and could move with a proper review.
- Older tickets have unreliable timestamps, so the money estimate uses only the
  ~9.5 months (14 Sept 2025 – 30 June 2026) since the current helpdesk went live.

## Suggested next steps

1. Have one Tier-2 agent read a fresh sample (about 150) of the re-categorised
   tickets and confirm or correct them.
2. Ask Billing and Logistics leads how many tickets are really handed across
   teams, and look at backlog or time spent, not just counts.
3. Decide the hires after that, and ask Finance whether the ₹350 credits are
   actually being paid.
