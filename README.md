# Vireo Audio — Support Ticket Categorization & Business Impact

A reproducible pipeline that re-categorizes Vireo Audio's support tickets
from their actual text, checks the categorization against a small AI-reviewed
first-pass sample, and uses the result to check the client's own proposed
basis for a headcount decision — plus a separate, policy-grounded
quantified business case (first-response SLA-breach cost).

## 1. Project overview

Vireo Audio (Bengaluru-based consumer-audio/wearables company) asked for:
monthly ticket breakdowns by category and by team, evidence that
categorization works, a quantified business outcome with a real ₹ number,
and a check on their proposed rule for where the next two support hires go
("whichever team has the most volume").

**Headline finding:** of the 2,564 tickets the intake bot tagged
`Billing & Payments` (all 11,780 tickets processed, not a sample), the
rule-based re-read of the ticket text assigns 898 (35.0%) to Billing and
1,666 (65.0%) to a different category; 1,049 (40.9%) go to Delivery &
Shipping. These are rule-based predictions, not verified ground truth. They
suggest raw Billing volume overstates Billing work, so volume alone is an
incomplete basis for the two-hire rule. See `memo/priya_memo.md` for the full,
non-technical version.

## 2. Business problem

Full framing (client's original ask, and every number's derivation) is in
`docs/methodology.md`. Short version: the bot sets each ticket's category
from a keyword-based intake flow before the real issue is known, and agents
"rarely" correct it (per the project's email thread). In the supplied
tickets.csv the bot category maps 1:1 to assigned_team for four of the seven
teams (Billing, Logistics, Returns Desk, Escalations & Warranty; verified by
crosstab), so a bad tag doesn't just
mislabel a report row — it puts the ticket in the wrong team's queue.

## 3. Solution architecture

```
data/            Source CSVs (as supplied, untouched)
src/
  config.py      Paths, taxonomy, and every cost/SLA constant (traceable to support-policy.pdf)
  load_data.py   CSV loading with dtype/date parsing
  clean_data.py  Dedup, PK validation, bad-date-order flagging
  categorize.py  Rule-based keyword categorizer (zero-cost, see docs/methodology.md §4)
  validate.py    Fixed-sample check against AI-reviewed first-pass labels
  analyze.py     Monthly/team aggregation, workload proxy, SLA-breach business case
  report.py      CSV/JSON/chart output writers
scripts/
  run_pipeline.py   Thin wrapper around run.py (same behavior)
tests/           pytest suite — real assertions, not "does it run"
outputs/         Generated by the pipeline (see below)
memo/            One-page non-technical memo for Priya Raman
docs/            methodology.md, ai_usage.md
run.py           Entry point
```

Rule-based keyword scoring was chosen over an LLM or a trained ML model
because no ground-truth labels exist in the supplied data (a trained model
needs labels from somewhere), the ticket text is short and keyword-dense
(exactly where rules are competitive with ML), and rules are free,
deterministic, and auditable by a non-data-scientist. Full reasoning in
`docs/methodology.md` §4.

## 9. Expected outputs

Running `python run.py` populates `outputs/`:

- `categorized_tickets.csv` — every ticket with `predicted_category`,
  confidence score/margin, and whether it disagrees with the bot's tag
  (raw `customer_message`/`agent_notes` text is **not** included in this
  export — see the data-handling note in `docs/methodology.md`)
- `monthly_category.csv`, `monthly_team.csv` — monthly breakdowns
- `team_volume_summary.csv` — the client's literal requested metric
- `team_workload_summary.csv`, `tickets_per_agent.csv` — workload beyond
  raw volume
- `validation_report.json` — AI-reviewed first-pass agreement, sample size, seed,
  error rate, reviewer type, limitations and failure modes
- `business_impact.json` — SLA-breach cost calculation, scenario, and the
  Billing-tag reclassification audit
- `charts/*.png` — 5 charts (monthly category, monthly team, tickets/agent,
  the Billing reclassification breakdown, SLA breach rate by channel)

## 10. Categorization methodology

See `docs/methodology.md` §4-5. Short version: keyword-scored rules across
11 categories reused verbatim from the bot's own taxonomy; customer_message and agent_notes are scored
together; routing phrases naming the receiving "Escalations & Warranty" team
are removed first. Result: **94.0% agreement (141/150, 9 errors) on a fixed
150-ticket sample against AI-reviewed first-pass labels. This is not an
independent human audit** — the labels were assigned by the AI assistant that
built the classifier, and only the 12 originally-flagged tickets have stored
labels (see `docs/methodology.md` §10).

## 11. Validation methodology

See `docs/methodology.md` §5. Seed 42, n=150, taxonomy v1.0-2026-09. The 12
tickets the original AI review flagged as wrong have fixed true labels and are
re-compared against the live prediction every run (3 now pass after the
routing/cancellation fixes; 9 remain). The other 138 were judged correct once
and are not re-checked, so a new regression among them would not be detected.
Remaining failure modes: promo/discount disputes (taxonomy gap), vocabulary
gaps, and resolution-note keywords outweighing the stated symptom.

## 12. Business metric methodology

See `docs/methodology.md` §7. First-response SLA-breach rate (10.9% = 839 of 7,728
helpdesk-era tickets, measured) × the policy-stated ₹350 credit
(support-policy.pdf §3), annualized → **≈ ₹370,873/year (≈ ₹92,718/quarter)
estimated policy-based liability**, not confirmed cash paid. A halved-rate
scenario in business_impact.json is illustrative only, not a prediction.

## 13. Cost calculation

**Zero paid API calls; ₹0 either way.** See `submission-form.md` question 2

See `docs/methodology.md` §10 for the full list. Headlines: 94% AI-reviewed first-pass agreement

## 16. Reproducibility

- `src/config.py` pins `RANDOM_SEED = 42` (used for the validation sample)
  and `TAXONOMY_VERSION` / model name, both recorded in every output file.
- The categorizer is fully deterministic (no LLM calls) — the same input
  always produces the same `predicted_category` for a given code version.
- The 12 flagged tickets' true labels are hard-coded in `src/validate.py`;
  whether each is an error is recomputed against the live prediction each
  run, so fixing or reintroducing one of those 12 cases moves `n_errors`. A
  new failure outside those 12 is not detected without re-review.
